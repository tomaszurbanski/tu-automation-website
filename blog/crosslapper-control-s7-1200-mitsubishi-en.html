Crosslapper Control with Siemens S7-1200 and Mitsubishi Drives

Tomasz UrbańskiJune 20269 min read
Language version: PL · DE · EN

A crosslapper (German Kreuzleger) is a machine used in nonwoven and technical-textile lines. From a thin material web entering from one side, it lays a multi-layer batt of a defined width transversely. The web runs through a system of aprons and two carriages — upper and lower — while a perpendicular take-off belt removes the finished batt. It sounds simple, yet it is one of the most demanding motion applications in the industry.

The challenge is not a single drive, but synchronizing several axes at once so that the laydown rate stays constant, the batt edge is straight, and the material is neither stretched nor crumpled.

Why crosslapper control is demanding

Within one cycle the lower carriage must accelerate and decelerate sharply at each end of the table — yet the amount of material laid per unit length must remain constant. This requires continuous, coordinated computation of the speeds of all web-transport elements in real time, for variable production settings.

Kinematic relationships

The key quantities and how they interrelate:

  • Infeed speed — the speed at which the web enters the machine; the reference for all other speeds.
  • Apron 1 — feeds material from the infeed to the upper carriage.
  • Apron 2 — transfers material from the upper carriage to the lower carriage.
  • Upper carriage — moves transversely at half the stroke of the lower carriage; its motion keeps the apron loop at a constant length.
  • Lower carriage — lays the material onto the table, travelling back and forth across the full laydown width.
  • Take-off belt — removes the finished batt perpendicular to the carriage motion; its speed results from the infeed speed and the number of layers.
  • Laydown width — defines the travel range of the lower carriage.
  • Number of layers — affects the batt thickness and directly the take-off belt speed.

These quantities are not independent — changing one setting (e.g. laydown width or number of layers) immediately recomputes the speed profiles of the carriages and belts. It is exactly this web of dependencies that determines batt quality.

Control platform

  • Controller: Siemens S7-1200 — compact, with enough performance to coordinate the motion and handle the laydown technology.
  • Drives: Mitsubishi — servo/inverters executing the speed profiles of the individual axes.
  • Key point: precise synchronization of carriages and belts — it determines a straight edge and a constant basis weight.

The carriage motion profile (e.g. S-curve instead of trapezoid) directly affects vibration, peak drive loads and the quality of the laid batt edge.
